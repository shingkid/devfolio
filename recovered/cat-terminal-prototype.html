Choices to react to

  • Font swapped to Commit Mono. Claude's actual UI runs on custom in-house faces (Anthropic Sans/Serif/Mono) that aren't licensed for outside use — Commit Mono is the closest open-license spirit: a plain, engineered, no-flourish coding face, same idea as a brand commissioning its own type.
  • Two weights, not four. Commit Mono ships Regular and Bold only — which is genre-correct too; a real terminal config doesn't have a fine weight ramp.
  • Dark is near-black with a lilac accent — a true dark ground, not a fully saturated violet-black, so the lilac reads as a clear accent rather than blending into the background.
  • Light is a pale cream with a pinker purple accent — lighter ground than the first pass, and the accent shifted from lavender toward magenta-violet (315° vs dark mode's 300°) for more presence against the cream.
  • Follows your system setting by default — the toggle above (top right) lets you force light or dark to compare them directly; click it again to go back to auto.
  • Three semantic colors, purposefully assigned — green marks shipped work and the current role, amber marks awards, blue marks papers and prototypes. They tag real information on the project/experience entries, not random decoration, and the lilac/purple accent stays reserved for the interface itself (prompt, links, cursor).
  • The status dot went from lilac to green — a small, real convention (green = connected/active) borrowed from actual terminal UIs, and a legitimate second use for the new palette.
  • There's a fish, and that's the whole plot. Whoever holds the terminal's corner has the fish and is quietly nibbling on it (a real periodic head-dip, not just breathing) — British Shorthair/Scottish Fold proportions, canvas-drawn, no image assets, sized up from earlier passes so the fish actually reads. Click the resident or press space to pet it between bites. Once an intruder reaches its swipe or bite, it's holding the fish in its mouth for the rest of the scuffle — carries it home if it wins the spot.
  • A visitor has a personality — the ginger bounds in fast and bouncy; the tuxedo creeps in with its head held low to the ground the whole way (creep, pause, creep, pause, creep, pause, a tense wiggle, then a leap), trying not to be seen. Either can show up regardless of theme — it's chance for the first visit, then they strictly take turns.
  • Arrivals have a punchline. The ginger's bound ends in a paw swipe at the seal point's face — a claw-mark flash lands on whoever's cheek gets hit, since the leg motion alone was too subtle to read — and the seal point swipes right back before stalking off, sneering, walking backward the whole way. The tuxedo's leap ends in an attempted love bite, which is exactly as well-received as you'd expect.
  • Then the roles swap. The visitor settles into the now-empty corner and lounges there; the seal point re-enters mimicking whichever approach it just saw, and the visitor mirrors its own earlier reaction back — a full call-and-response before it resets and waits for the next encounter. Fully random timing throughout, so it reads as a surprise rather than a metronome.
  • Chips are load-bearing, not decoration — most visitors are recruiters who won't guess command names.
  • Now a real filesystem, not just a flat command list. Every section is a markdown file (try ls, cd projects, cat pawkit) rendered Glow-style — but about/projects/etc. still work directly as shortcuts, so nobody has to learn Unix to get around. The prompt tracks your directory (~$ → ~/projects$) once you start navigating.

This prototype is a static approximation for direction sign-off — commands here are pre-written responses, not the real xterm.js build. Say the word on any choice (accent hue, prompt string, chip style, pacing) and I'll adjust before the real build starts.
